# Buyback Taxation & Transaction Costs (India)

Buyback taxation in India underwent a monumental shift under the **Finance Act, 2024**, effective **October 1, 2024**. Every report must accurately reflect these rules.

---

## 1. The Post-October 1, 2024 Tax Regime

Prior to October 1, 2024, buyback proceeds were tax-free for shareholders under Section 10(34A), and companies paid a buyback distribution tax under Section 115QA. **This has ended.**

Under the current regime:

### Rule 1: Deemed Dividend Treatment (Section 2(22)(f))
The entire gross payout received from the company for accepted shares is treated as **Deemed Dividend** and taxed at the shareholder's applicable **Income Tax Slab Rate**:

$$\text{Tax on Buyback Proceeds} = \text{Gross Consideration Received} \times \text{Applicable Slab Rate}$$

- **TDS**: 10% TDS is deducted at source by the company under Section 194 for resident shareholders if the payout exceeds ₹5,000. (Form 15G / 15H can be submitted for nil TDS if total income is below the exemption limit).

### Rule 2: Capital Loss Creation on Accepted Shares
The original purchase cost (cost of acquisition) of the accepted shares is **not** deductible against the deemed dividend income. Instead, it is treated as a **Capital Loss**:
- If held for $\le 12$ months: **Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL)**
- If held for $> 12$ months: **Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL)**

$$\text{Allowable Capital Loss} = \text{Accepted Shares } (A) \times \text{Purchase Price } (P_{\text{buy}})$$

### Rule 3: Capital Loss Setoff & Carry-Forward
- **STCL** can be set off against any taxable Short-Term or Long-Term Capital Gains (STCG or LTCG) from equity or mutual fund trades in the same financial year.
- Unadjusted capital losses can be carried forward for up to **8 assessment years**.
- *Net Tax Benefit*: If the investor has existing capital gains (e.g., STCG taxed at 20%), the capital loss generated by the buyback saves real tax on those gains:
  $$\text{Tax Shield Saved} = \text{Allowable Capital Loss} \times 20\% \text{ (STCG rate)}$$

## 2. Tax Impact Comparison Table

Illustration for a retail investor tendering ₹1,00,000 worth of shares (Cost = ₹80,000, Buyback Payout = ₹1,00,000):

| Component | 0% Tax Slab (Exempt) | 10% Tax Slab | 30% Tax Slab |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross Buyback Payout** | ₹1,00,000 | ₹1,00,000 | ₹1,00,000 |
| **Original Purchase Cost** | ₹80,000 | ₹80,000 | ₹80,000 |
| **Gross Gain** | ₹20,000 | ₹20,000 | ₹20,000 |
| **Tax on Deemed Dividend** | ₹0 | -₹10,000 | -₹30,000 |
| **Capital Loss Created (STCL)** | ₹80,000 loss | ₹80,000 loss | ₹80,000 loss |
| **STCL Tax Shield Saved (at 20% STCG)** | ₹0 | +₹16,000 | +₹16,000 |
| **Net Post-Tax Profit (with STCG setoff)** | **+₹20,000** | **+₹26,000** | **+₹6,000** |
| **Net Post-Tax Profit (without STCG setoff)**| **+₹20,000** | **+₹10,000** | **-₹10,000** |

> **Key Takeaway**: For investors in the 20%–30% tax brackets, buyback arbitrage is most profitable if they have capital gains elsewhere in their portfolio against which to set off the resulting capital loss.

## 3. Standard Transaction Charges (NSE/BSE)

When buying and tendering shares, include the following friction costs:

| Charge Type | Rate / Flat Fee | Applicable On |
|---|---|---|
| **Brokerage** | ₹20 flat or 0.05% per order | Purchase & Unaccepted Sale |
| **Securities Transaction Tax (STT)** | 0.1% | Secondary purchase & Tender delivery |
| **Exchange Transaction Charges** | ~0.00345% | Buy & Sell turnover |
| **SEBI Turnover Fee** | ₹10 per crore (0.0001%) | Both legs |
| **State Stamp Duty** | 0.015% (₹150 per crore) | Purchase leg |
| **DP / Tender Processing Charges** | ₹15 to ₹30 + GST per company | Debit of shares for tender |
| **GST** | 18% on (Brokerage + Txn Charges + DP) | Service fees |

**Rule of Thumb**: Total round-trip frictional transaction costs typically amount to approximately **0.30% to 0.40%** of the invested capital.
